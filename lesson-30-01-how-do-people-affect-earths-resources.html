📖 The Lesson
Five factors, measured with tools
⏱ 15 min
🌡️
Section 1
Five Factors Make the Weather

Meteorologists describe the weather with five factors. Air temperature is how hot or cold the air is. Air pressure is how hard the air is pushing down. Humidity is how much water vapor is in the air. Wind has a speed and a direction. Precipitation is any water falling from clouds.

Each factor has its own tool. A thermometer measures temperature. A barometer measures air pressure. A hygrometer measures humidity. An anemometer measures wind speed, and a wind vane shows direction. A rain gauge collects precipitation.

🤔
Stop and Think
"Which of the five factors can you feel on your skin? Which one can you only measure with a tool?"
⏲️
Section 2
Reading the Clues

The factors work together. When air pressure falls, air is rising. Rising air cools, and its water vapor condenses into clouds. If humidity is high too, rain or snow is likely. When pressure rises, air sinks and dries out, so skies usually clear.

Wind matters too. It carries warm, wet air from the ocean, or cold, dry air from the north. A change in wind direction often means the weather is about to change.

🧪
Stop and Reason
"The barometer is falling and the air feels sticky. What weather would you predict, and why?"
🌧️
Section 3
Four Kinds of Precipitation

Rain is liquid drops. Snow is ice crystals that stay frozen all the way down. Sleet starts as snow, partly melts in a warm layer of air, then refreezes into ice pellets in cold air near the ground. Hail forms inside thunderstorms. Strong winds toss ice up and down in the cloud, adding layer after layer, until it is too heavy and falls.

The temperature of the air between the cloud and the ground decides which one lands. That's why one storm can bring rain to one town and sleet or snow to the next.

🗺️
Stop and Connect
"Hail can fall on a hot summer day, but sleet only falls when it's cold near the ground. Why?"
🗺️
Section 4
Weather Across Environments and Climate Zones

Weather can be very different from one environment to another. A swamp or wetland is usually warm and humid. A desert is dry, with hot days and cool nights. High on a mountain, the air is colder, even in summer. Temperature and humidity are two of the biggest differences.

Climate is the usual weather of a place over many years. Scientists group places into climate zones. Three things help decide a climate zone: latitude (how far a place is from the equator), elevation (how high it is), and how close it is to the ocean or other bodies of water. Places near the equator are warmer. Higher places are colder. Places near the ocean have milder temperatures.

🗺️
Stop and Connect
"Miami and a town high in the Rocky Mountains can have very different weather on the same day. Which climate-zone clues explain why?"
🔑 Key Concept

Air temperature, air pressure, humidity, wind, and precipitation together determine the weather at a place and time. Precipitation can fall as rain, snow, sleet, or hail, depending on the temperature of the air it falls through. Different environments, like swamps, deserts, and mountains, have different temperature and humidity. Latitude, elevation, and closeness to water shape each climate zone.

Mind-Blowing Fact
The largest hailstone ever measured in the United States fell in Vivian, South Dakota, on July 23, 2010. It was 8 inches across and weighed almost 2 pounds, according to the National Weather Service.
Go Deeper — Why sleet and freezing rain are different

Sleet and freezing rain both start as snow that melts in a warm layer of air. The difference is the cold layer near the ground. If it is deep enough, the drops refreeze into pellets before landing: that's sleet. If it is too shallow, the drops land as liquid and freeze on contact with cold roads and branches: that's freezing rain.

Both make roads slippery, but freezing rain can coat trees and power lines in heavy ice.

A barometer shows the air pressure falling fast, and the humidity is high. What weather is most likely coming?
AClear, dry skies
BClouds and precipitation
CNo change at all
✅ Correct! Falling pressure means rising air, which cools and forms clouds. With lots of water vapor in the air, rain or snow is likely.
❌ Not quite. Falling pressure means air is rising and cooling. What happens to water vapor when air cools? Think about clouds.
Falling snow melts in a warm layer of air, then refreezes into ice pellets before it hits the ground. What is it?
ASleet
BHail
CRain
✅ Correct! Sleet is snow that partly melts, then refreezes into small ice pellets on the way down.
❌ Not quite. Hail forms inside thunderstorm clouds, not by refreezing on the way down. Rain would still be liquid. What's left?
