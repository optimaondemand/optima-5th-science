Part A — Multiple Choice (select the best answer)
Question 1
A student built a model to show how matter moves through an ecosystem. After new evidence showed that fungi also decompose matter, she added fungi to her model. What does this revision show about scientific models?
A Models are always wrong the first time and must be rebuilt from scratch
B Models are revised when new evidence provides a better or more complete explanation
C A model that needs revision means the student did not understand the lesson
D Scientists only revise models when their teacher tells them to
Question 2
Which of the following is the best example of a fair test?
A Testing two different brands of fertilizer on plants that got different amounts of water
B Changing only the amount of light a plant gets while keeping all other conditions the same
C Observing a plant for one day and concluding it needs more water
D Measuring plant height once and comparing it to a picture in a textbook
Question 3
A CER (Claim-Evidence-Reasoning) explains scientific thinking. Which part explains WHY the evidence supports the claim?
A The Claim — states the answer
B The Evidence — provides the data
C The Reasoning — connects the science behind the evidence to the claim
D The Reflection — restates what was learned
Question 4
A student notices that her earlier explanation of conservation of matter said 'some mass disappears when a substance dissolves.' After running investigations, she revised her explanation. What does this show?
A She made an error and her teacher had to correct her
B Her original explanation was based on limited evidence and was improved through investigation
C Scientists should not change their explanations once they are written
D Dissolving is a special case where conservation of matter does not apply
Part B — Short Response (write in complete sentences)
Question 5
Choose one model you built or revised during the year. Describe what the model showed, what evidence it was based on, and how the model helped you understand something you could not see directly.
Question 6
Describe one investigation from the year where data surprised you or changed your thinking. What was your prediction? What did the data actually show? What did you conclude?
Question 7
Write a complete CER for this question: 'Does mass change when matter undergoes a physical change?' Use data from the unit as your evidence.
Question 8
Describe one idea you held at the start of the year that you now think differently about. What evidence changed your mind? What do you understand now that you did not before?
