Part A — Multiple Choice (select the best answer)
Question 1
Two stars are observed from Earth. Star A appears very bright; Star B appears dim. A scientist discovers that Star B is actually much larger than Star A. What is the most likely explanation for the difference in appearance?
A Star B produces less energy than Star A
B Star A is closer to Earth than Star B, making it appear brighter
C The size of a star always determines how bright it looks from Earth
D Star B must be behind a cloud in Earth's atmosphere
Question 2
Earth's rotation causes day and night. Which statement best describes what happens during one full rotation?
A Earth orbits completely around the Sun once every 24 hours
B One side of Earth faces the Sun (day) while the other is in shadow (night), completing one full spin in about 24 hours
C The Moon blocks sunlight from reaching Earth every night
D Earth stops rotating and the Sun moves across the sky
Question 3
Most of Earth's water is found in:
A Rivers and lakes — fresh, accessible water on the surface
B Underground aquifers — stored beneath the soil
C The oceans — salt water that covers about 97% of Earth's total water
D Ice caps and glaciers — the largest freshwater reserve
Question 4
A community wants to reduce its use of nonrenewable energy sources. Which solution best uses criteria and constraints?
A Telling people to use less electricity without offering an alternative
B Comparing solar panels and wind turbines using cost, reliability, and local conditions before choosing
C Switching everyone to a single energy source without testing it first
D Using more nonrenewable sources because they are easier to find
Part B — Short Response (write in complete sentences)
Question 5
Explain what happens to a water droplet during the water cycle. Trace it from the ocean through at least three steps: evaporation, condensation, and precipitation.
Question 6
Describe how two of Earth's four spheres (geosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphere, biosphere) interact with each other. Give a specific example for each interaction.
Question 7
Only about 3% of Earth's water is fresh water, and most of that is frozen in ice caps. What does this tell us about the availability of drinking water? Why should this matter to people who live far from the ocean?
Question 8
A student says, 'We should just dig more wells to solve the freshwater shortage.' What is one strength and one weakness of this solution? What criteria or constraints would you use to evaluate it?
